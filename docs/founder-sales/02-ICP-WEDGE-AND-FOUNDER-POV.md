# 2) ICP Wedge + Founder POV One-Pager

## ICP Wedge (Primary)

**Ideal buyer titles:**
Chief Compliance Officer (CCO), Director of Compliance, Compliance Manager, or the ops-owner/principal who wears the compliance hat at a small firm.

**Company type:**
SEC-registered RIAs, 1–20 advisers, ~$50M–$1B AUM. Typically independent or boutique firms without a dedicated compliance technology stack.

**Trigger event ("why now"):**
- SEC exam notice received (or overdue / expecting one)
- Recent regulatory enforcement action in their peer group ("that could be us")
- New adviser hired → more meetings to supervise → documentation burden jumps
- CCO turnover — new person inherits a messy compliance file
- Annual compliance review reveals gaps in supervision documentation

**Pain (in their words):**
- "I spend hours after every meeting writing up notes and linking them to our supervision file."
- "If the SEC walked in tomorrow, I'd need a weekend to pull together our documentation."
- "We use a Word template and a spreadsheet — it works until it doesn't."
- "I can't prove we followed up on the flag from that client meeting three months ago."
- "Every adviser documents differently. I have no consistency."

**Impact:**
- **Time:** 3–8 hours/week spent on post-meeting documentation per CCO
- **Risk:** Deficiency findings, enforcement actions, reputational damage, personal liability for CCO
- **Money:** Exam remediation costs ($10K–$100K+); potential fines; opportunity cost of CCO time on manual work vs. higher-value compliance strategy

**Exclusion criteria (NOT for):**
- Large RIAs with entrenched enterprise compliance platforms (Smarsh, Global Relay, ComplySci) and procurement processes
- Firms that don't conduct client/adviser meetings (rare, but exists)
- Firms unwilling to use any cloud-based or AI-assisted tooling for compliance
- Anyone looking for "auto-compliance" with no human review

## ICP Wedge (Secondary): Compliance Consultants / Outsourced CCO Firms

**Why they matter early:**
They serve 5–50+ RIA clients each. Winning one consultant = distribution across their entire book. They feel the documentation pain at *scale* — multiplied across every client firm.

**Trigger:** New client onboarded with poor documentation; or preparing multiple firms for exams simultaneously.

**Pain:** "I'm doing the same manual documentation work across 15 clients. I need leverage."

## Founder POV (problem-first narrative)

### What's changing / why now

The SEC's examination priorities increasingly emphasize *supervision evidence* — not just policies on paper, but proof that firms actually follow them in practice. At the same time, RIA meeting volume is growing (more clients, more advisers, more touchpoints), and the documentation burden is growing with it.

Small RIAs are caught in the middle: too small for enterprise compliance platforms, too regulated to wing it with Word docs and spreadsheets.

### What breaks in the old way

Manual post-meeting documentation is:
- **Inconsistent** — every adviser writes notes differently (or not at all)
- **Incomplete** — flags, follow-ups, and decisions fall through the cracks
- **Unlinked** — there's no evidence chain connecting a meeting to the supervision action taken
- **Unscalable** — a CCO supervising 10 advisers can't manually review and document every meeting
- **Fragile under pressure** — when an exam notice arrives, it takes days to reconstruct what happened

### The cost of doing nothing

- An SEC exam with documentation gaps leads to deficiency findings, remediation demands, and potential enforcement
- CCO time stays trapped in low-value manual work instead of proactive compliance strategy
- As the firm grows, the documentation problem compounds — each new adviser multiplies the burden
- "One exam scramble costs more than a year of Comply Vault."

### Your insight (why you see it)

You've built compliance-grade workflow software in regulated digital healthcare — where AI-assisted decisions still require human review and sign-off. That same philosophy applies here: AI does the heavy lifting (transcription, extraction, flagging, linking), but the CCO retains full review authority and sign-off. You understand audit trails, retention controls, access management, and evidence linking because you've built them before in environments where regulators expect it.

### What you do differently (not a features list)

Comply Vault doesn't replace the CCO's judgment — it eliminates the manual labor *around* that judgment. Upload a meeting recording, get a speaker-labeled transcript with extracted decisions, flags, and follow-ups, linked to the supervision evidence chain. The CCO reviews, signs off, and exports an exam-ready pack. Minutes, not hours.

### Proof points (3)

1. **Product proof:** Live demo that turns a meeting recording into an exam-ready evidence pack in <10 minutes
2. **Workflow proof:** Built-in audit trail, version history, and evidence linking — designed for examiner scrutiny
3. **Risk proof:** Human review/sign-off model + configurable retention rules + access controls — compliance-grade by design, not bolted on
